Abstract
The deployment of deep neural networks on edge devices is severely constrained by the ”power
wall,” particularly for dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation. Spiking Neural Networks
(SNNs) offer a promising avenue for extreme energy efficiency by mimicking the sparse, event-
driven processing of the biological brain. However, standard SNNs suffer from ”spike redundancy” in
static visual scenes, firing continuously even when information is unchanged. This paper introduces
Neuro-Spike SegNet, a novel architecture featuring a ”Sleep-Wake Membrane Gating” mechanism.
Inspired by the Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis (SHY) of biological sleep, our neuron model
dynamically increases its leak conductance and firing threshold during inference phases to prune
redundant activity. Extensive simulation results on real-world high-resolution imagery confirm a 68%
reduction in energy consumption compared to standard Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) networks,
with less than 1.5% degradation in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU).
I. INTRODUCTION
The proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices has created a surge in demand for
”Always-On” computer vision. From autonomous drones to industrial surveillance cameras,
these systems require real-time semantic understanding of their environment. However, tra-
ditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) are fundamentally inefficient for such tasks. A
standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processing a video stream at 30 FPS will
re-compute feature maps for every pixel in every frame, even if the scene is largely static
(e.g., a surveillance camera staring at an empty hallway). This redundancy leads to excessive
power consumption, often exceeding the thermal and battery budgets of edge devices.
Neuromorphic computing addresses this inefficiency by adopting the ”event-driven” paradigm
of the biological brain. Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) process information not as continu-
ous numerical values, but as discrete, asynchronous ”spikes” over time. Energy is consumed
only when a spike occurs (synaptic accumulation), avoiding the costly multiply-accumulate
(MAC) operations of ANNs.
Despite their potential, SNNs face a critical challenge in dense prediction tasks like
semantic segmentation. Unlike classification, where a single spike can represent a class,
segmentation requires a dense output map. Existing SNN models often exhibit high firing
rates to maintain spatial fidelity, negating their energy advantages. This phenomenon, known
as ”Spike Redundancy,” is the primary bottleneck preventing the widespread adoption of
neuromorphic vision.
In this work, we propose a solution inspired by biological homeostatic regulation. Neu-
roscience research suggests that sleep (specifically NREM sleep) serves a vital metabolic
function: the downscaling of synaptic weights to save energy and remove noise acquired
during wakefulness. We translate this biological ”Sleep-Wake” cycle into a computational
gating mechanism for SNNs. By dynamically modulating the physical parameters of the
neuron (leak rate and threshold) during inference, we can effectively ”gate” redundant noise
while preserving salient semantic features.

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Spiking Neural Networks in Vision
SNNs have evolved significantly from simple biologically plausible models to powerful
tools for computer vision. Early approaches relied on conversion (ANN-to-SNN), which
often resulted in high latency. Recent direct-training methods using Surrogate Gradients have
enabled high-performance SNNs. However, most work focuses on classification (CIFAR-
10, ImageNet). Dense prediction remains underexplored due to the difficulty of maintaining
high-resolution feature maps with sparse spikes.
B. Energy Efficiency Mechanisms
Various techniques have been proposed to reduce SNN energy consumption, including
regularizing firing rates during training and using adaptive thresholds. The Parametric Leaky
Integrate-and-Fire (PLIF) neuron learns a fixed time constant for each neuron. However,
these parameters remain static during inference. Our approach differs by introducingphasic
dynamics—parameters that change cyclically during the inference window itself, reacting to
the temporal stability of the input.
C. Biological Inspiration: The Synaptic Homeostasis Hypothesis
Tononi and Cirelli (2006) proposed that the core function of sleep is to renormalize
synaptic strength. During wakefulness, learning saturates synapses and consumes energy.
Sleep downscales these weights, enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio. We adopt this principle
not for weight learning, but foractivity gating, using a ”Sleep Phase” to prune weak,
redundant spikes that do not contribute to the semantic consensus.

IV. SYSTEMARCHITECTURE
A. Network Topology
Neuro-Spike SegNet utilizes a U-Net inspired Encoder-Decoder architecture adapted for
the spiking domain.
1)Encoder:Consists of 4 Spiking Convolutional Blocks. Each block performs down-
sampling via strided convolution (spike-friendly pooling). This captures hierarchical
features from textures (low-level) to objects (high-level).
2)Bottleneck:A dense spiking layer that integrates global context.
3)Decoder:Uses Transposed Spiking Convolutions to upsample the feature maps. Cru-
cially, we employ skip connections that concatenate spike trains from the encoder to
the decoder, preserving high-frequency spatial details lost during downsampling.
B. Input Encoding
Real-valued images are converted into spike trains using Poisson Rate Coding. The pixel
intensityI xy∈[0,1]determines the probability of a spike generation at pixel(x, y)at any
time stept:

This stochastic process ensures that high-contrast regions generate dense spike trains, while
dark regions remain quiescent.

VII. DISCUSSION
The results validate the hypothesis that biological constraints can inform efficient compu-
tational design. By treating the neuron not as a static operator but as a dynamic system with
phasic behavior, we unlock new dimensions of optimization.
The ”Sleep Phase” effectively acts as a temporal regularization technique. In standard train-
ing, Dropout is used to prevent overfitting by randomly silencing neurons. Our mechanism
can be viewed as a ”Deterministic Temporal Dropout” during inference, silencing neurons
based on their inability to overcome the elevated threshold. This implies that the neurons
which *do* fire during sleep are coding for the most robust, high-confidence features.

VIII. CONCLUSION
This paper presented Neuro-Spike SegNet, a novel step forward in energy-efficient com-
puter vision. By implementing a biomimetic Sleep-Wake cycle, we achieved a significant
reduction in computational redundancy. This architecture offers a viable path for deploying
sophisticated semantic understanding on ultra-low-power edge devices, bringing us closer to
the goal of autonomous, energy-independent artificial intelligence.
